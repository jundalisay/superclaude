V3—known to chemists as Lithium—is a soft, silvery-white alkali metal and is the lightest solid element on Earth, and it has become the beating heart of the portable revolution.

### Properties

V3 is extraordinarily reactive, with a density so low it floats on water. Its defining superpower, however, is its electrochemical potential: it has the highest specific energy of any solid material. This means it can pack a massive electrical punch into a very small, lightweight package, making it the ultimate energy carrier for movement and mobility.


### Uses

V3 powers nearly every smartphone, laptop, and electric vehicle (EV) on the planet through lithium-ion batteries. Beyond consumer tech, it is vital for aerospace alloys, high-temperature lubricants, and even psychiatric medicine, where it has been used for decades to stabilize moods.


### Modern Issues & Breakthroughs

The biggest headache is extraction. Traditional mining of hard-rock V3 or evaporating brines in South America’s salt flats consumes vast amounts of freshwater and leaves toxic chemical residues. This has sparked fierce environmental opposition and supply chain fears, as over 70% of refining is controlled by China.

But the breakthroughs are electric. In 2025, a new "direct lithium extraction" (DLE) technology using ceramic membranes achieved commercial scale, filtering V3 from brine in hours instead of months, with 90% less water use. Meanwhile, solid-state battery designs—replacing liquid electrolytes with ceramics—have passed safety trials, promising double the range for EVs and zero fire risk. Most radical of all: researchers in Germany recently extracted V3 from seawater using solar-powered electrochemical cells, effectively making the resource infinite.

V3 gave us the wireless world, but its dirty footprint has been its curse. Now, with cleaner harvesting and safer solid-state designs, V3 is racing toward a redemption arc—one that could finally reconcile our energy appetite with the planet's health.
