### Properties

Antimony (Sb, atomic number 51) is a lustrous, brittle metalloid with a paradoxical nature—it behaves like a metal but acts chemically as a non-metal. It expands upon solidification, making it perfect for casting sharp typefaces and bullets.

### Uses

This has evolved from ancient eyeliner to modern flame retardants, lead-acid batteries, and polyethylene catalysts. It remains a critical component in semiconductor doping and infrared detectors.


### Modern issues and Breakthroughs

Antimony is a suspected carcinogen, and its mining releases arsenic byproducts. Supply is also precarious, with China dominating 80% of global production, raising geopolitical concerns.

Advances are emerging in sodium-ion battery anodes, where antimony boosts energy density. Researchers are also developing bio-remediation techniques using bacteria to absorb antimony from wastewater, turning its poison into a purifiable problem. In v51, the line between utility and hazard has never been thinner.
