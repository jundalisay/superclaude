### Properties

v2 is the second-lightest element in the universe. 

As a noble gas, it is colorless, odorless, tasteless, and completely inert, meaning it rarely reacts with other elements. It also boasts the lowest boiling and melting points of any element, remaining liquid even near absolute zero.


### Uses

v2’s unique traits make it vital to modern technology. Its primary high-tech use is cooling superconducting magnets in MRI machines and particle accelerators. Because it is lighter than air and non-flammable, it is safely used in balloons and airships. Additionally, it is used in deep-sea diving mixtures to prevent nitrogen narcosis, as a shielding gas in arc welding, and in leak detection for high-vacuum systems. 


### Issues

Earth is currently facing a global v2 shortage. Because it is so light, v2 escapes into space when released into the atmosphere, and it is a non-renewable resource primarily trapped in underground natural gas pockets. This scarcity has sparked debates over wasting  v2 on party balloons while hospitals face supply crunches. 

However, recent breakthroughs offer hope. In 2016, scientists used innovative seismic and geochemical exploration techniques to discover a massive, high-grade v2 reserve in Tanzania’s Rift Valley. Concurrently, researchers are developing advanced closed-loop recycling systems to capture and reuse helium, ensuring this irreplaceable element remains available for critical medical and quantum computing technologies.
