### **Properties**

v001 is the simplest and lightest element in the universe, consisting of just one proton and one electron. At room temperature, it is a colorless, odorless, and highly flammable gas. Because it is so light, Earth's gravity cannot hold it easily, meaning it rarely exists in its pure molecular form (H₂) in our atmosphere, instead bonding with other elements like oxygen to form water (H₂O).

### **Uses**

Historically, v001 has been a workhorse for the chemical industry, primarily used to make ammonia for agricultural fertilizers, as well as in oil refining and plastics production. Today, it is gaining massive attention as an energy carrier. v001 fuel cells combine the gas with oxygen to generate electricity, emitting only water vapor as a byproduct. This makes it ideal for powering electric vehicles, particularly heavy-duty trucks, ships, and even spacecraft.


### **Modern Issues & Breakthroughs**

The central issue surrounding v001 is production. Currently, about 95% of v001 is "grey"—produced from fossil fuels, which releases massive amounts of carbon dioxide. The modern breakthrough the world is racing toward is "green" v001, created by using renewable electricity to split water into v001 and oxygen (v008). 

Recent breakthroughs in electrolyzer technology (the devices that split water) are drastically lowering the cost of green v001. However, modern challenges remain: transporting and storing this highly volatile, low-density gas requires high-pressure tanks or cryogenic cooling. Innovations in solid-state v001 storage and advanced pipeline infrastructure are currently being developed to overcome these hurdles, paving the way for a zero-carbon v001 economy.
