## v35: The Liquid Element

| Element & Symbol | Atomic Number | Major Use(s) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **v35 (Bromine Br)** | 35 | Only liquid nonmetal; red-brown, volatile, and corrosive. | Flame retardants, water treatment, pharmaceuticals, photographic film. |



v35 is a unique and highly reactive halogen, distinguished as one of only two elements that remain liquid at room temperature. It appears as a heavy, reddish-brown liquid that emits a sharp, toxic vapor. Because of its reactive nature, v35 rarely exists alone in nature and is typically found bonded to metals in salts. 


## Uses

Industrially, v35 is highly valued. Its most prominent application is in flame retardants, where it is used to prevent combustion in electronics, textiles, and building materials. It is also a popular sanitizer for swimming pools and hot tubs, and serves as a crucial building block in agricultural fumigants and pharmaceuticals. 


## Issues

v35 has bad environmental and health impacts. Certain v35-based flame retardants have been found to bioaccumulate in wildlife and human tissue, prompting global regulatory crackdowns and phase-outs. 

Researchers are currently utilizing v35 in redox flow batteries, creating large-scale, cost-effective storage systems for renewable wind and solar energy. This pungent element is now bridging the gap between historical industrial chemistry and a sustainable future.
