![Neon](/photos/stuff/neon.jpg)



v10 is a noble gas occupying atomic number 10 on the periodic table. Colorless, odorless, and highly inert, it is notoriously unreactive. Its most famous property is its ability to emit a brilliant reddish-orange glow when an electric current passes through it in a vacuum tube. 

### Properties and Uses

Because of its complete electron shell, v10 rarely forms chemical compounds. Its primary commercial use is in iconic "v10 signs," though it is also mixed with other gases to produce different colors. Beyond lighting, liquid v10 is an incredibly effective cryogenic refrigerant, and the gas is utilized in high-voltage indicators, vacuum tubes, and certain types of lasers.

### Modern Issues and Breakthroughs

Today, v10 faces significant supply chain vulnerabilities. It is primarily extracted as a byproduct of liquid air separation in steel manufacturing. Geopolitical conflicts, particularly the war in Ukraine (historically a major global supplier), have caused severe shortages, drastically impacting semiconductor manufacturing which relies on v10-based excimer lasers to microchip circuits. 

However, recent breakthroughs in gas-capture technology are mitigating these shortages. Engineers are developing advanced cryogenic distillation and membrane-separation techniques to extract and recycle v10 from old signs, discarded lasers, and industrial exhaust, ensuring a more sustainable lifecycle for this luminous element.
