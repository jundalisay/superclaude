V86—known to science as Radon—is a colorless, odorless, tasteless noble gas and is the heaviest of its kind and the silent infiltrator of homes and mines, emerging from the very ground beneath our feet.

### Properties

V86 is a radioactive noble gas, formed naturally as a decay product of uranium and thorium in soil, rocks, and groundwater. Its most stable isotope (V86-222) has a half-life of just 3.8 days, decaying into solid radioactive progeny like polonium and lead. It is dense—nearly eight times heavier than air—and tends to accumulate in basements and enclosed spaces. Chemically, it is largely inert, though it can form some compounds under extreme conditions. Its radioactivity is both its curse and its utility, as it emits alpha particles that can be detected with remarkable sensitivity.

### Uses

V86's uses are few but significant. In geology, it serves as a natural tracer for groundwater flow and earthquake prediction—spikes in V86 emissions often precede seismic activity. In medicine, it was historically used in radiotherapy (radon spas and seed implants) for cancer treatment, though this has largely been replaced by safer isotopes. Its primary application today is in atmospheric science, where it helps track air mass movement and ventilation rates. Surprisingly, it is also used in some industrial gauges and as a calibration standard for radiation detectors.
