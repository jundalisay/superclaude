**Convection** is the bulk, linear movement of gas driven by **temperature differences** — hot gas rises, cool gas sinks. It is the **Mid Displacement force** in Superphysics' Gas Relationalities — the linear, translational action of gas masses at intermediate scales.

Unlike diffusion (molecular, micro), convection moves **entire volumes of gas** in organized, directional flow. Unlike turbulence (chaotic, rotational), convection is **ordered and linear** — a coordinated rising and falling of gas masses.


## The Equation: Newton's Law of Cooling & Heat Transfer

### Convective Heat Transfer

\[
Q = hA(T_s - T_\infty)
\]

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( Q \) | Heat transfer rate | \( \text{W} \) |
| \( h \) | Convective heat transfer coefficient | \( \text{W}/(\text{m}^2 \cdot \text{K}) \) |
| \( A \) | Surface area | \( \text{m}^2 \) |
| \( T_s \) | Surface temperature | \( \text{K} \) |
| \( T_\infty \) | Ambient gas temperature | \( \text{K} \) |

### Rayleigh Number (Onset Criterion)

\[
Ra = \frac{g \beta \Delta T L^3}{\nu \alpha}
\]

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \( g \) | Gravitational acceleration |
| \( \beta \) | Thermal expansion coefficient |
| \( \Delta T \) | Temperature difference |
| \( L \) | Characteristic length |
| \( \nu \) | Kinematic viscosity |
| \( \alpha \) | Thermal diffusivity |

| \( Ra \) Range | Behavior |
|---|---|
| \( Ra < 1700 \) | No convection (pure conduction) |
| \( Ra > 1700 \) | **Convection begins** |
| \( Ra > 10^9 \) | Turbulent convection |

### Buoyancy Force (Driving Mechanism)

\[
F_b = \rho g \beta \Delta T \cdot V
\]

Where \( V \) is the volume of displaced gas.

## Key Properties of Gas Convection

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Buoyancy-driven** | Hot gas is less dense → rises; cool gas is denser → sinks |
| **Linear** | Bulk gas moves in organized, directional paths |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Larger \( \Delta T \) → stronger convection |
| **Gravity-dependent** | No gravity → no natural convection |
| **Two types** | Natural (buoyancy) and forced (fan/pump) |
| **Carries heat** | Dominant heat transfer mode in gases |

### Types of Convection

| Type | Driver | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural** | Buoyancy (temperature difference) | Hot air balloon |
| **Forced** | External fan/pump | Air conditioner |
| **Mixed** | Both | Wind over a warm surface |



### Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Convection Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Hot air balloon** | Burner heats air inside → less dense → rises, lifting the balloon |
| **2. Sea breeze** | Land heats faster than sea → hot air rises over land → cool sea air flows in |
| **3. Land breeze** | Sea cools slower than land → warm air rises over sea → cool land air flows out |
| **4. Radiator heating** | Hot radiator warms air → rises → cool air sinks → circulation loop |
| **5. Thunderstorm formation** | Warm moist air rises rapidly → cools → condenses → storm clouds |
| **6. Chimney draft** | Hot combustion gases rise → pull fresh air in from below |
| **7. Boiling water** | Hot water at bottom rises → cool water sinks → convection currents |
| **8. Atmospheric circulation** | Equator heats air → rises → moves poleward → cools → sinks |
| **9. Hair dryer** | Forced convection blows hot air onto wet hair |
| **10. Computer cooling** | Fans force air convection over CPU heat sinks |



### Convection vs. Other Gas Forces

| Force | Type | Direction | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | Displacement (linear) | High → low concentration | Micro |
| **Turbulence** | Cohesion (rotational) | Chaotic, swirling | Micro |
| **Convection** | Displacement (linear) | Hot → cold (buoyancy-driven) | Mid |
| **Vortex** | Cohesion (rotational) | Spiral, wrapping | Mid |
| **Air Pressure** | Displacement (linear) | High → low pressure | Macro |
| **Coriolis** | Cohesion (rotational) | Deflecting, wrapping | Macro |

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is gas convection? | Bulk, linear movement of gas driven by temperature differences |
| What law describes it? | **Newton's Law of Cooling**: \( Q = hA(T_s - T_\infty) \) |
| What type of force? | **Mid Displacement** (linear, Rule 1 + 3) |
| What drives it? | Buoyancy from temperature differences |
| Real examples? | Hot air balloons, sea breeze, thunderstorms, radiators |
| What's its rotational counterpart? | **Vortex / Eddy** (Mid Cohesion) |



**Bottom line:** Gas convection is the **organized, rising-and-falling circulation** of gas masses driven by heat — the Mid Displacement force that pairs with vortex's rotational wrapping in Superphysics' Gas Relationalities.
