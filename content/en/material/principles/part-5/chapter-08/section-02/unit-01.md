**Diffusion** is the spontaneous, linear movement of gas molecules from a region of **higher concentration** to a region of **lower concentration**. It is the **Micro Displacement force** in Superphysics' Gas Relationalities — the linear, translational action of individual gas particles through the aetherspace.

Unlike turbulence (rotational/chaotic), diffusion is **directional and linear** — molecules march from dense to sparse until equilibrium is reached.

## The Equation: Fick's Law

### Fick's First Law (Steady-State)

\[
J = -D \frac{dC}{dx}
\]

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( J \) | Diffusion flux (amount flowing per area per time) | \( \text{mol}/(\text{m}^2 \cdot \text{s}) \) |
| \( D \) | Diffusion coefficient (depends on gas, temperature, pressure) | \( \text{m}^2/\text{s} \) |
| \( \frac{dC}{dx} \) | Concentration gradient (change in concentration over distance) | \( \text{mol}/\text{m}^4 \) |
| \( - \) | Negative sign = flow is **down** the gradient (high → low) | — |

### Fick's Second Law (Time-Dependent)

\[
\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial x^2}
\]

This predicts **how concentration changes over time** as diffusion proceeds.

## Key Properties of Gas Diffusion

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Passive** | No external energy needed — driven by entropy |
| **Linear** | Molecules move in straight paths between collisions |
| **Random walk** | Each molecule zigzags, but net flow is directional |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → faster diffusion |
| **Inversely pressure-dependent** | Higher \( P \) → slower diffusion |
| **Mass-dependent** | Lighter gases diffuse faster (Graham's Law) |

### Graham's Law of Effusion/Diffusion

\[
\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}
\]

Lighter gas diffuses faster. Example: helium \( (M = 4) \) diffuses ~2.6× faster than oxygen \( (M = 32) \).



## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Diffusion Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Perfume in a room** | Fragrance molecules spread from the bottle (high concentration) to the entire room (low concentration) |
| **2. Oxygen entering blood** | \( O_2 \) diffuses from lung alveoli (high \( O_2 \)) into capillaries (low \( O_2 \)) |
| **3. Carbon dioxide leaving blood** | \( CO_2 \) diffuses from blood (high \( CO_2 \)) into lungs (low \( CO_2 \)) |
| **4. Natural gas leak** | Methane spreads through air from the leak point outward |
| **5. Ammonia and HCl experiment** | Two gases in a tube meet and form a white ring — closer to the heavier gas (HCl) |
| **6. Aerosol spray** | Propellant gas diffuses into surrounding air |
| **7. Plant respiration** | \( CO_2 \) diffuses into leaves through stomata; \( O_2 \) diffuses out |
| **8. Helium balloon deflating** | Helium atoms diffuse through the balloon's microscopic pores |
| **9. Smell of cooking** | Aroma molecules diffuse from kitchen to other rooms |
| **10. Landfill methane** | Methane diffuses through soil and into the atmosphere |

## Diffusion vs. Other Gas Forces

| Force | Type | Direction | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | Displacement (linear) | High → low concentration | Micro |
| **Turbulence** | Cohesion (rotational) | Chaotic, swirling | Micro |
| **Convection** | Displacement (linear) | Hot → cold (buoyancy-driven) | Mid |
| **Vortex** | Cohesion (rotational) | Spiral, wrapping | Mid |
| **Air Pressure** | Displacement (linear) | High → low pressure | Macro |
| **Coriolis** | Cohesion (rotational) | Deflecting, wrapping | Macro |
