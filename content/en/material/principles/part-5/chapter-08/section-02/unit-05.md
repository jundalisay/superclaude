**Eddies** are swirling, rotational structures that form within a flowing gas or fluid, where coherent vortices emerge and persist despite the surrounding chaotic motion. They are the **Mid Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Gas Relationalities — the rotational, structure-forming action of the gaseous medium as it organizes itself through aetherspace and space.

Unlike convection (linear/displacement), eddies are **rotational and self-sustaining** — gas curls back on itself, forming stable loops that trap momentum and energy.

## The Equation: Vorticity and Eddy Formation

### Vorticity (Micro Origins, Macro Manifestation)

$$
\omega = \nabla \times \mathbf{v}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \omega $$ | Vorticity (local rotation rate) | $$ \text{s}^{-1} $$ |
| $$ \mathbf{v} $$ | Velocity field | $$ \text{m}/\text{s} $$ |
| $$ \nabla \times $$ | Curl operator | — |

### Navier–Stokes Vorticity Form (Cohesion in Motion)

$$
\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\omega = (\omega \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v} + \nu \nabla^2 \omega
$$

Here \( (\omega \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v} \) is the **vortex stretching term** — the mechanism that intensifies eddies. Viscosity \( \nu \nabla^2 \omega \) diffuses rotation, acting as cohesive damping.

### Reynolds Number (Transition to Eddy Formation)

$$
Re = \frac{\rho v L}{\mu}
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Re  | Reynolds number (ratio of inertial to viscous forces) | — |
| $$ \rho $$ | Gas density | $$ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 $$ |
| $$ v $$ | Flow velocity | $$ \text{m}/\text{s} $$ |
| $$ L $$ | Characteristic length | $$ \text{m} $$ |
| $$ \mu $$ | Dynamic viscosity | $$ \text{Pa} \cdot \text{s} $$ |

When Re > 2000, laminar flow breaks into eddies — cohesion emerges from displacement.

### Kolmogorov Scale (Smallest Eddy Size)

$$
\eta = \left( \frac{\nu^3}{\varepsilon} \right)^{1/4}
$$

Where $$ \varepsilon $$ is the energy dissipation rate. Below $$ \eta $$, viscosity smooths out rotation.



## Key Properties of Eddies

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Fluid curls around a local axis — pure cohesion |
| **Self-sustaining** | Vortices feed on shear and persist until viscosity dissipates them |
| **Hierarchical** | Large eddies spawn smaller eddies (energy cascade) |
| **Momentum-trapping** | Eddies carry trapped mass and momentum over distances |
| **Pressure-linked** | Low pressure at vortex core (Bernoulli) |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → lower \( \nu \) → eddies persist longer |
| **Scale-dependent** | Eddy size determines lifetime and energy content |

### Energy Cascade (Richardson)

$$
E(k) \propto k^{-5/3}
$$

Energy flows from large eddies (small \( k \)) to small eddies (large \( k \)) until viscosity dissipates it as heat.



## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Eddies Work |
|---|---|
| **1. Smoke rings** | A puff of smoke forms a toroidal vortex that travels intact |
| **2. Turbulence behind aircraft** | Wingtip vortices spiral behind planes, dangerous to following aircraft |
| **3. Ocean eddies** | Large swirling currents (e.g., Gulf Stream rings) trap warm water |
| **4. Jupiter's Great Red Spot** | A persistent anticyclonic eddy larger than Earth |
| **5. Kármán vortex street** | Alternating eddies shed behind a cylinder in flow |
| **6. Tornadoes** | Intense vertical eddies formed from wind shear |
| **7. Coffee cup swirls** | Stirring creates eddies that persist after spoon removed |
| **8. Atmospheric cyclones** | Low-pressure systems with rotating eddy structure |
| **9. River rapids** | Rocks create standing eddies in flowing water |
| **10. Dust devils** | Small, short-lived eddies formed by surface heating |
