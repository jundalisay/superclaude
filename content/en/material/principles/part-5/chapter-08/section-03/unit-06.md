**Surface Tension** is the rotational, contractive force that causes the surface of a liquid to behave like an elastic membrane, pulling inward and minimizing surface area. It is the **Macro Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Liquid Relationalities — the large-scale, structure-forming action of the liquid medium as it organizes itself through aetherspace and the outer intraaetherspace.

Unlike viscous force (linear/displacement), surface tension is **rotational and contractive** — molecules at the surface curl inward, forming a coherent skin that resists penetration and minimizes area.

## The Equation: Surface Tension Fundamentals

### Surface Tension Definition (Force per Length)

$$
\gamma = \frac{F}{L}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \gamma $$ | Surface tension | $$ \text{N}/\text{m} $$ |
| F | Force acting perpendicular to a line on the surface | $$ \text{N} $$ |
| L | Length of the line | $$ \text{m} $$ |


### Surface Energy (Work to Create New Surface)

$$
W = \gamma \Delta A
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| W | Work done to create new surface | $$ \text{J} $$ |
| $$ \gamma $$ | Surface tension | $$ \text{N}/\text{m} $$ |
| $$ \Delta A $$ | Change in surface area | $$ \text{m}^2 $$ |


### Young–Laplace Equation (Pressure Across Curved Surface)

$$
\Delta P = \gamma \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \right)
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \Delta P \) | Pressure difference across the surface | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( R_1, R_2 \) | Principal radii of curvature | \( \text{m} \) |

### Laplace Pressure for a Spherical Droplet

$$
\Delta P = \frac{2\gamma}{r}
$$

Where \( r \) is the droplet radius. Smaller droplets have higher internal pressure — the rotational cohesive force is stronger at smaller scales.

### Capillary Rise Height (Jurins Law)

$$
h = \frac{2\gamma \cos\theta}{\rho g r}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| h  | Capillary rise height | $$ \text{m} $$ |
| $$ \theta $$ | Contact angle | $$ \text{rad} $$ or $$ \degree $$ |
| \( \rho \) | Liquid density | \( \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 \) |
| \( g \) | Gravitational acceleration | \( \text{m}/\text{s}^2 \) |
| \( r \) | Capillary radius | \( \text{m} \) |

## Key Properties of Surface Tension

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Surface molecules curl inward, creating a coherent skin |
| **Contractive** | Minimizes surface area — pulls liquid into spheres |
| **Cohesive** | Arises from unbalanced cohesive forces at the surface |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → lower \( \gamma \) (thermal motion disrupts cohesion) |
| **Curvature-linked** | Pressure increases with curvature (Young–Laplace) |
| **Scale-dependent** | Dominant at small scales; negligible at large scales |
| **Elastic-like** | Surface behaves as a stretchable membrane |

### Eötvös Rule (Temperature Dependence)

$$
\gamma V^{2/3} = k_E (T_c - T)
$$

Where \( V \) is molar volume, \( k_E \) is the Eötvös constant, and \( T_c \) is the critical temperature. As \( T \to T_c \), surface tension vanishes.

### Kelvin Equation (Vapor Pressure Over Curved Surface)

$$
\ln\left(\frac{P}{P_0}\right) = \frac{2\gamma V_m}{rRT}
$$

Where \( V_m \) is molar volume and \( P_0 \) is the saturation vapor pressure over a flat surface. Curvature alters cohesive equilibrium.

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Surface Tension Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Water droplets** | Cohesion pulls water into spherical beads |
| **2. Soap bubbles** | Surface tension forms spherical films; surfactants stabilize them |
| **3. Water striders** | Insect legs dent the surface without breaking it |
| **4. Paper clips floating** | Surface tension supports metal clips on water |
| **5. Meniscus in a glass** | Water curls up at the edges due to surface tension |
| **6. Capillary action** | Surface tension draws liquid up narrow tubes |
| **7. Raindrops** | Surface tension forms droplets during condensation |
| **8. Mercury beads** | High \( \gamma \) makes mercury form tight spheres |
| **9. Detergents** | Surfactants lower \( \gamma \), allowing water to wet surfaces |
| **10. Lotus effect** | High \( \gamma \) causes water to bead and roll off leaves |
