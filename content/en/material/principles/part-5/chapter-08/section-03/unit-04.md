**Capillary Action** is the spontaneous, rotational climbing or depression of liquid within narrow spaces, arising from the interplay of adhesion, cohesion, and surface tension. It is the **Micro Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Liquid Relationalities — the rotational, structure-forming action of the liquid medium as it organizes itself through aetherspace and the outer intraaetherspace.

Unlike interfacial tension (linear/displacement), capillary action is **rotational and cohesive** — liquid curls into concave or convex menisci and draws itself along narrow channels in a self-sustaining loop.

## The Equation: Capillary Fundamentals

### Young–Laplace Equation (Pressure Across Curved Meniscus)

$$
\Delta P = \gamma \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \right)
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \Delta P $$ | Pressure difference across meniscus | $$ \text{Pa} $$ |
| $$ \gamma $$ | Surface tension | $$ \text{N}/\text{m} $$ |
| $$ R_1, R_2 $$ | Principal radii of curvature | $$ \text{m} $$ |

### Capillary Rise Height (Jurins Law)

$$
h = \frac{2\gamma \cos\theta}{\rho g r}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( h \) | Capillary rise (or depression) height | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( \gamma \) | Surface tension | \( \text{N}/\text{m} \) |
| \( \theta \) | Contact angle between liquid and tube | \( \text{rad} \) or \( \degree \) |
| \( \rho \) | Liquid density | \( \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 \) |
| \( g \) | Gravitational acceleration | \( \text{m}/\text{s}^2 \) |
| \( r \) | Capillary radius | \( \text{m} \) |

### Washburn Equation (Time-Dependent Capillary Flow)

$$
L^2 = \frac{\gamma r \cos\theta}{2\mu} t
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( L \) | Penetration distance | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( \mu \) | Dynamic viscosity | \( \text{Pa} \cdot \text{s} \) |
| \( t \) | Time | \( \text{s} \) |

### Laplace Pressure (Curvature-Induced Cohesion)

$$
P_{\text{Laplace}} = \frac{2\gamma}{r}
$$

This pressure pulls liquid into the capillary, sustaining the rotational cohesive draw.

## Key Properties of Capillary Action

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Liquid curls into meniscus, forming a curved interface |
| **Cohesive** | Self-sustaining draw through narrow channels |
| **Adhesion-dependent** | Liquid must wet the tube $$ \theta < 90\degree $$ for rise |
| **Cohesion-dependent** | Strong cohesive forces → depression $$ \theta > 90\degree $$ |
| **Radius-dependent** | Smaller r → greater capillary rise |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher T → lower $$ \gamma $$ → less rise |
| **Self-reinforcing** | Curvature creates pressure that draws more liquid |

### Contact Angle (Wetting Condition)

$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\gamma_{sg} - \gamma_{sl}}{\gamma_{lg}}
$$

Where

$$ \gamma_{sg} $$, $$ \gamma_{sl} $$, and $$ \gamma_{lg} $$ are solid–gas, solid–liquid, and liquid–gas interfacial tensions. This determines whether capillary action rises or depresses.



## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Capillary Action Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Water rising in a straw** | Narrow tube draws water up against gravity |
| **2. Plant water transport** | Xylem vessels pull water from roots to leaves |
| **3. Paper towel absorbing spills** | Cellulose fibers wick liquid through narrow pores |
| **4. Ink in a fountain pen** | Capillary channels feed ink to the nib |
| **5. Tear film in eyes** | Lacrimal fluid spreads across cornea via capillaries |
| **6. Soil water retention** | Small soil pores hold water against gravity |
| **7. Chromatography** | Solvent climbs paper, separating chemical compounds |
| **8. Candle wick** | Melted wax rises through the wick to feed the flame |
| **9. Concrete moisture** | Water migrates through microscopic pores |
| **10. Microfluidic chips** | Capillary action drives liquid through tiny channels without pumps |
