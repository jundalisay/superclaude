**Buoyant Force** is the upward, directed force exerted by a fluid on an immersed body, arising from the pressure difference between the bottom and top of the object. It is the **Mid Displacement force** in Superphysics' Liquid Relationalities — the linear, translational action of the liquid medium as it redistributes itself around a displaced body through aetherspace and the outer intraaetherspace.

Unlike cohesive adhesive (rotational/cohesive), buoyant force is **directional and linear** — fluid pushes upward along a straight line to restore equilibrium.

## The Equation: Buoyancy Fundamentals

### Archimedes' Principle (Displacement Law)

$$
F_b = \rho_f g V_d
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ F_b $$ | Buoyant force (upward) | $$ \text{N} $$ |
| $$ \rho_f $$ | Fluid density | $$ \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 $$ |
| g | Gravitational acceleration | $$ \text{m}/\text{s}^2 $$ |
| $$ V_d $$ | Displaced fluid volume | $$ \text{m}^3 $$ |

### Pressure Gradient Origin (Micro Displacement)

$$
F_b = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} P \, d\mathbf{A} = -\rho_f g V_d \, \hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \oint_{\mathcal{S}} $$ | Surface integral over the body | — |
| P | Hydrostatic pressure at depth | $$ \text{Pa} $$ |
| $$ d\mathbf{A} $$ | Outward area element | $$ \text{m}^2 $$ |
| $$ \hat{\mathbf{z}} $$ | Vertical unit vector (upward) | — |

### Hydrostatic Pressure Difference (Displacement Driver)

$$
\Delta P = \rho_f g h
$$

Where h is the depth difference between the bottom and top of the immersed body. The larger pressure at the bottom pushes upward — pure linear displacement.

### Floating Condition (Equilibrium)

$$
\rho_f V_d = \rho_b V_b
$$

Where \( \rho_b \) is body density and \( V_b \) is total body volume. The fraction submerged is:

$$
\frac{V_d}{V_b} = \frac{\rho_b}{\rho_f}
$$



## Key Properties of Buoyant Force

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Directional** | Always acts vertically upward |
| **Linear** | Pushes along a straight line opposing gravity |
| **Volume-dependent** | Depends on displaced fluid volume, not body mass |
| **Density-dependent** | Heavier fluids produce greater buoyancy |
| **Depth-independent** | Net buoyancy same at any depth (for incompressible fluid) |
| **Pressure-driven** | Arises from pressure gradient across body height |
| **Restoring** | Returns floating bodies to equilibrium position |


### Stability Condition (Metacenter)

$$
GM = \frac{I}{V_d} - BG
$$

Where

$$ GM $$ is metacentric height, I is moment of inertia of waterplane, and \( BG \) is center-of-gravity to center-of-buoyancy distance. Stable if GM > 0.



## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Buoyant Force Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Ships floating** | Hull displaces water; buoyancy balances ship's weight |
| **2. Submarines diving** | Ballast tanks fill with water, reducing buoyancy |
| **3. Hot air balloons** | Heated air is less dense; displaced cool air pushes balloon up |
| **4. Icebergs** | ~90% submerged because ice density is ~90% of water |
| **5. Swimming** | Human body displaces water; buoyancy reduces effective weight |
| **6. Life jackets** | Low-density foam displaces water, adding upward force |
| **7. Helium balloons** | Helium displaces air; buoyancy exceeds balloon weight |
| **8. Dead Sea floating** | High salinity → high \( \rho_f \) → greater buoyancy |
| **9. Fish swim bladders** | Gas-filled bladders adjust buoyancy at different depths |
| **10. Hydrometers** | Float at depth calibrated to liquid density |
