**Torque** is the rotational force that causes an object to twist or rotate about an axis, arising from a force applied at a distance from that axis. It is the **Macro Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities — the large-scale, structure-forming action at the boundary between the aetherspace and space.

Unlike friction (linear/displacement), torque is **rotational and twist-inducing** — it curls the material around an axis, creating coherent angular displacement that binds the structure into a torsional configuration.

## The Equation: Torque Fundamentals

### Torque Definition (Moment of Force)

$$
\tau = r F \sin\theta
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \tau $$ | Torque (moment of force) | $$ \text{N} \cdot \text{m} $$ |
| \( r \) | Distance from axis to force application | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( F \) | Applied force | \( \text{N} \) |
| \( \theta \) | Angle between \( r \) and \( F \) | \( \text{rad} \) or \( \degree \) |

### Vector Form (Cross Product)

$$
\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}
$$

Where \( \mathbf{r} \) is the position vector and \( \mathbf{F} \) is the force vector. Torque is perpendicular to both.

### Rotational Analog of Newton's Second Law

$$
\tau = I \alpha
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( I \) | Moment of inertia | \( \text{kg} \cdot \text{m}^2 \) |
| \( \alpha \) | Angular acceleration | \( \text{rad}/\text{s}^2 \) |

### Torsional Shear Stress (Internal Distribution)

$$
\tau_{\text{max}} = \frac{T r}{J}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( T \) | Applied torque | \( \text{N} \cdot \text{m} \) |
| \( r \) | Radial distance from axis | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( J \) | Polar moment of inertia | \( \text{m}^4 \) |

### Angle of Twist (Torsional Deformation)

$$
\phi = \frac{T L}{G J}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \phi \) | Angle of twist | \( \text{rad} \) |
| \( L \) | Length of shaft | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( G \) | Shear modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( J \) | Polar moment of inertia | \( \text{m}^4 \) |

### Torsional Strain Energy (Stored Cohesion)

$$
U = \frac{1}{2} T \phi
$$

This energy is stored in the twisted material and released when torque is removed.

## Key Properties of Torque

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Causes angular displacement about an axis |
| **Twist-inducing** | Creates torsional deformation in materials |
| **Cohesive** | Binds structure into a coherent twisted configuration |
| **Distance-dependent** | Greater \( r \) → greater torque for same force |
| **Angle-dependent** | Maximum when \( F \perp r \) |
| **Axis-dependent** | Depends on choice of rotation axis |
| **Material-dependent** | \( G \) and \( J \) determine resistance to twist |

### Polar Moment of Inertia (Geometric Resistance)

$$
J = \frac{\pi}{2} r^4
$$

For a solid circular shaft. Larger radius dramatically increases torsional resistance.

### Power Transmission (Rotational Cohesion in Motion)

$$
P = \tau \omega
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( P \) | Power | \( \text{W} \) |
| \( \tau \) | Torque | \( \text{N} \cdot \text{m} \) |
| \( \omega \) | Angular velocity | \( \text{rad}/\text{s} \) |

### Torsional Pendulum (Oscillatory Cohesion)

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{\kappa}}
$$

Where \( \kappa \) is the torsional spring constant. The period depends on rotational inertia and restoring torque.

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Torque Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Wrench tightening a bolt** | Long handle multiplies torque to turn bolt |
| **2. Steering wheel** | Torque about steering column turns wheels |
| **3. Drilling a hole** | Drill bit applies torque to cut through material |
| **4. Opening a door** | Pushing far from hinges creates torque |
| **5. Bicycle pedals** | Force on pedals creates torque to turn chainring |
| **6. Wind turbine** | Wind creates torque on blades, generating power |
| **7. Torsion bar suspension** | Twisted bar stores energy, returns to shape |
| **8. Screwdriver** | Torque drives screw into material |
| **9. Earth's rotation** | Tidal torque gradually slows Earth's spin |
| **10. Torque wrench** | Measures precise torque for critical fasteners |
