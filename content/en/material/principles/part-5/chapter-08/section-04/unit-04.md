**Elastic Force** is the rotational, restorative force that arises when a material is deformed and attempts to return to its original shape, storing and releasing energy through internal structural reorganization. It is the **Micro Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities — the rotational, structure-forming action at the microscopic scale between the aetherspace and space.

Unlike normal force (linear/displacement), elastic force is **rotational and restorative** — atomic bonds stretch and rotate, curling back toward equilibrium when the deforming force is removed.

## The Equation: Elasticity Fundamentals

### Hooke's Law (Linear Elastic Regime)

$$
F = -kx
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| F | Elastic restoring force | $$ \text{N} $$ |
| k | Spring constant (stiffness) | $$ \text{N}/\text{m} $$ |
| x | Displacement from equilibrium | $$ \text{m} $$ |
| \( - \) | Negative sign = force opposes displacement | — |

### Elastic Potential Energy (Stored Cohesion)

$$
U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2
$$

Where `U` is the potential energy stored in the deformed material. This energy is released when the material returns to equilibrium.

### Stress–Strain Relation (Bulk Elasticity)

$$
\sigma = E \varepsilon
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \sigma $$ | Stress (force per area) | $$ \text{Pa} $$ |
| E | Young's modulus | $$ \text{Pa} $$ |
| $$ \varepsilon $$ | Strain (deformation per length) | dimensionless |

### Shear Modulus (Rotational Elasticity)

$$
\tau = G \gamma
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \tau \) | Shear stress | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( G \) | Shear modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \gamma \) | Shear strain | dimensionless |

### Bulk Modulus (Volumetric Elasticity)

$$
\Delta P = -B \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( B \) | Bulk modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \Delta P \) | Pressure change | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \frac{\Delta V}{V_0} \) | Volumetric strain | dimensionless |

### Poisson's Ratio (Lateral Coupling)

$$
\nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{lat}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{axial}}}
$$

Where \( \nu \) is Poisson's ratio. Elastic deformation in one direction couples to rotation in perpendicular directions.

## Key Properties of Elastic Force

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Atomic bonds stretch and rotate during deformation |
| **Restorative** | Always directed toward equilibrium position |
| **Reversible** | Returns to original shape when force removed |
| **Energy-storing** | Stores potential energy in deformed bonds |
| **Linear** | Proportional to displacement (Hookean regime) |
| **Material-dependent** | \( E \), \( G \), \( B \) vary widely across materials |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → lower modulus (generally) |

### Elastic Limit (Yield Point)

Beyond the elastic limit, deformation becomes plastic — permanent and non-restorative. The material no longer returns to its original shape.

### Anharmonicity (Nonlinear Elasticity)

$$
U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 + \frac{1}{6}\beta x^3 + \cdots
$$

At large displacements, the restoring force becomes nonlinear. Thermal expansion arises from this anharmonicity.

### Viscoelasticity (Time-Dependent Cohesion)

$$
\sigma(t) = \int_0^t E(t - \tau) \dot{\varepsilon}(\tau) \, d\tau
$$

Where \( E(t) \) is the relaxation modulus. Materials like polymers exhibit both elastic and viscous behavior.

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Elastic Force Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Spring** | Compressed or stretched spring returns to natural length |
| **2. Rubber band** | Stretched rubber snaps back to original shape |
| **3. Bungee cord** | Elastic recoil decelerates jumper safely |
| **4. Trampoline** | Elastic mat stores and releases energy to bounce |
| **5. Bow and arrow** | Bent bow stores elastic energy, launching arrow |
| **6. Ball bouncing** | Elastic deformation at impact returns ball to height |
| **7. Watch spring** | Coiled spring stores energy to power mechanism |
| **8. Cartilage** | Elastic tissue cushions joints and recovers shape |
| **9. Spider silk** | Elastic protein fibers absorb prey impact energy |
| **10. Atomic force microscopy** | Cantilever elasticity measures nanoscale forces |

| Real examples? | Springs, rubber bands, trampolines, bows, bouncing balls |
