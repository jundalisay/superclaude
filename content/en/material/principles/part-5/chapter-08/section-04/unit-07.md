**Torsion** is the rotational deformation that occurs when a material is twisted about its longitudinal axis by an applied torque, causing shear stress to distribute across its cross-section. It is the **Macro Cohesion force** in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities — the large-scale, structure-forming action at the boundary between the aetherspace and space.

Unlike friction (linear/displacement), torsion is **rotational and twist-inducing** — it curls the material around its axis, creating coherent angular displacement that binds the structure into a helical configuration.

## The Equation: Torsion Fundamentals

### Torsional Shear Stress (Internal Distribution)

$$
\tau = \frac{T r}{J}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \tau \) | Shear stress at radius \( r \) | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( T \) | Applied torque | \( \text{N} \cdot \text{m} \) |
| \( r \) | Radial distance from axis | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( J \) | Polar moment of inertia | \( \text{m}^4 \) |

### Maximum Shear Stress (At Outer Surface)

$$
\tau_{\text{max}} = \frac{T c}{J}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( c \) | Outer radius of shaft | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( \tau_{\text{max}} \) | Maximum shear stress | \( \text{Pa} \) |

### Polar Moment of Inertia (Geometric Resistance)

$$
J = \frac{\pi}{2} r^4
$$

For a solid circular shaft. For a hollow shaft:

$$
J = \frac{\pi}{2} (r_o^4 - r_i^4)
$$

Where \( r_o \) is outer radius and \( r_i \) is inner radius.

### Angle of Twist (Torsional Deformation)

$$
\phi = \frac{T L}{G J}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \phi \) | Angle of twist | \( \text{rad} \) |
| \( L \) | Length of shaft | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( G \) | Shear modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( J \) | Polar moment of inertia | \( \text{m}^4 \) |

### Torsional Strain Energy (Stored Cohesion)

$$
U = \frac{1}{2} T \phi = \frac{T^2 L}{2 G J}
$$

This energy is stored in the twisted material and released when torque is removed.

### Torsional Rigidity (Resistance to Twist)

$$
k_t = \frac{G J}{L}
$$

Where \( k_t \) is the torsional stiffness. Higher \( k_t \) means greater resistance to angular displacement.

## Key Properties of Torsion

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Rotational** | Material twists about its longitudinal axis |
| **Helical** | Cross-sections rotate progressively along length |
| **Cohesive** | Binds structure into a coherent twisted form |
| **Shear-dominated** | Internal stresses are primarily shear |
| **Radius-dependent** | Stress increases linearly with distance from axis |
| **Length-dependent** | Longer shafts twist more for same torque |
| **Material-dependent** | \( G \) and \( J \) determine torsional resistance |

### Torsional Failure Modes

| Material Type | Failure Mode |
|---|---|
| **Ductile** | Shear yield; twists permanently |
| **Brittle** | Fractures along 45° helical plane |
| **Composite** | Delamination and fiber breakage |

### Torsional Pendulum (Oscillatory Cohesion)

$$
T_{\text{period}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{k_t}}
$$

Where \( I \) is moment of inertia and \( k_t \) is torsional stiffness. The period depends on rotational inertia and restoring torque.

### Wave Propagation (Torsional Waves)

$$
v_t = \sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( v_t \) | Torsional wave velocity | \( \text{m}/\text{s} \) |
| \( G \) | Shear modulus | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \rho \) | Density | \( \text{kg}/\text{m}^3 \) |

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Torsion Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Driveshaft** | Transmits torque from engine to wheels, twisting along length |
| **2. Torsion bar suspension** | Twisted metal bar stores energy, returns to shape |
| **3. Screwdriver** | Twisting motion drives screw into material |
| **4. Wringing a towel** | Torsion squeezes water out through twisting |
| **5. Drilling a well** | Drill string twists to transmit torque to bit |
| **6. Guitar string tuning** | Tuning pegs apply torsion to adjust tension |
| **7. Torsional pendulum clock** | Twisted suspension regulates timekeeping |
| **8. Bridge cables** | Torsional forces from wind cause twisting oscillations |
| **9. Turbine shaft** | Transmits rotational power under torsional load |
| **10. DNA supercoiling** | Biological torsion stores energy in twisted strands |

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is torsion? | Rotational deformation from twisting about a longitudinal axis |
| What law describes it? | **Torsion Formula**: \( \tau = \frac{T r}{J} \); **Angle of Twist**: \( \phi = \frac{T L}{G J} \) |
| What type of force? | **Macro Cohesion** (rotational, Rule 2 + 4) |
| What drives it? | Applied torque; angular displacement about axis |
| Real examples? | Driveshafts, torsion bars, screwdrivers, wringing towels, DNA supercoiling |
| What's its displacement counterpart? | **Friction** (Macro Displacement) |


**Bottom line:** Torsion is the **rotational, helical twisting** of a material about its axis — the Macro Cohesion force that pairs with friction's linear sliding resistance in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities.
