**Tensile and Compression Forces** are the directed, linear forces that either pull a material apart (tension) or push it together (compression) along its axis. They are the **Mid Displacement force** in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities — the linear, translational action at the intermediate scale where the aetherspace and space interact through bulk material deformation.

Unlike shear (rotational/cohesive), tensile and compressive forces are **directional and linear** — they act along the material's axis, stretching or shortening it in a straight line.

## The Equation: Stress and Strain Fundamentals

### Normal Stress (Force per Area)

$$
\sigma = \frac{F}{A}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $$ \sigma $$ | Normal stress (tensile positive, compressive negative) | $$ \text{Pa} = \text{N}/\text{m}^2 $$ |
| $$ F $$ | Applied axial force | $$ \text{N} $$ |
| $$ A $$ | Cross-sectional area | $$ \text{m}^2 $$ |

### Normal Strain (Deformation per Length)

$$
\varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( \varepsilon \) | Normal strain | dimensionless |
| \( \Delta L \) | Change in length | \( \text{m} \) |
| \( L_0 \) | Original length | \( \text{m} \) |

### Hooke's Law (Linear Elastic Regime)

$$
\sigma = E \varepsilon
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| \( E \) | Young's modulus (stiffness) | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \sigma \) | Normal stress | \( \text{Pa} \) |
| \( \varepsilon \) | Normal strain | dimensionless |

### Axial Deformation (Displacement Formula)

$$
\Delta L = \frac{F L_0}{A E}
$$

This is the **direct displacement equation** — force stretches or compresses a material along its axis.

### Poisson's Ratio (Lateral Contraction)

$$
\nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{lat}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{axial}}}
$$

Where \( \nu \) is Poisson's ratio, \( \varepsilon_{\text{lat}} \) is lateral strain, and \( \varepsilon_{\text{axial}} \) is axial strain. Materials thin when stretched and thicken when compressed.

## Key Properties of Tensile and Compression Forces

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Directional** | Acts along the material's axis |
| **Linear** | Produces straight-line elongation or shortening |
| **Reversible** | Elastic deformation returns to original shape |
| **Irreversible** | Plastic deformation causes permanent change |
| **Material-dependent** | \( E \) varies widely (steel ~200 GPa, rubber ~0.01 GPa) |
| **Temperature-dependent** | Higher \( T \) → lower \( E \) (generally) |
| **Scale-dependent** | Dominant at structural scales |

### Stress–Strain Curve (Material Behavior)

| Region | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Elastic** | Linear; Hooke's Law applies |
| **Yield** | Onset of plastic deformation |
| **Plastic** | Permanent deformation |
| **Ultimate** | Maximum stress before failure |
| **Fracture** | Material breaks |

### Thermal Stress (Temperature-Induced Displacement)

$$
\sigma_{\text{thermal}} = -E \alpha \Delta T
$$

Where \( \alpha \) is the coefficient of thermal expansion and \( \Delta T \) is temperature change. Constrained materials develop stress when heated or cooled.

### Buckling (Compression Instability)

$$
F_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{(K L)^2}
$$

Where \( F_{\text{cr}} \) is critical buckling load, \( I \) is moment of inertia, and \( K \) is effective length factor. Compression can cause sudden lateral displacement.

## Real-Life Examples

| Example | How Tensile/Compression Works |
|---|---|
| **1. Suspension bridge cables** | Tensile forces pull cables straight, supporting deck weight |
| **2. Concrete pillars** | Compressive forces push down, supporting building loads |
| **3. Rubber band** | Tensile force stretches it; releases when force removed |
| **4. Rebar in concrete** | Steel reinforcement handles tensile forces; concrete handles compression |
| **5. Bicycle spokes** | Spokes are under tension, holding wheel rim in compression |
| **6. Bone structure** | Bones experience tension and compression during walking |
| **7. Crane cables** | Tensile forces lift heavy loads |
| **8. Spring** | Compression or tension stores elastic energy |
| **9. Guitar strings** | Tensile forces tune pitch; tighter = higher pitch |
| **10. Arch bridges** | Compression flows through arch; tension in tie rods |

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What are tensile/compression forces? | Linear axial forces pulling apart or pushing together |
| What law describes them? | **Hooke's Law**: \( \sigma = E \varepsilon \); **Axial Deformation**: \( \Delta L = \frac{F L_0}{A E} \) |
| What type of force? | **Mid Displacement** (linear, Rule 1 + 3) |
| What drives them? | Applied axial loads, thermal expansion, structural weight |
| Real examples? | Bridge cables, concrete pillars, rubber bands, bicycle spokes |
| What's its rotational counterpart? | **Shear** (Mid Cohesion) |



**Bottom line:** Tensile and compression forces are the **linear, axial push-pull** of materials — the Mid Displacement force that pairs with shear's rotational sliding cohesion in Superphysics' Solid Relationalities.
