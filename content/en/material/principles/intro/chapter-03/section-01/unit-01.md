The 1st Rule says:

> No identity moves into the place of another identity unless at the same time that other identity moves into the place of another identity.

We call this the no void rule.

This is most obvious in liquid dynamics and aerodynamics.


## $\sum_i \Delta x_i = 0$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x_i$ | position of identity $i$ (physical location, or metaphysical "place" — a market position, a social role, a country's status) |
| $\Delta x_i$ | the *change* in position of identity $i$ (how far and which way it moved) |
| $i$ | index labeling which identity — identity 1, identity 2, identity 3... |
| $\sum_i$ | "sum over all $i$" — add up the displacement of every identity in the system |
| $=0$ | the total adds up to zero — net displacement across the whole system is nothing; every move out is matched by a move in |


**Equation Meaning:** add up how far every identity moved, and it must total zero — nothing moves without something else moving to fill its place.


The 1st rule is one of the 2 movements described by Timeaus:

{{< q a="Timaeus" c="Timeaus by Plato" >}}
