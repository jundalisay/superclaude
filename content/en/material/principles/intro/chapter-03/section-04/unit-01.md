The 4th Rule is implemented by quantum mechanics.

 


## $\Delta v = \dfrac{F}{m}\cdot\dfrac{\epsilon}{|\omega_F-\omega_i|+\epsilon}$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\Delta v$ | the resulting change in the identity's state (velocity, opinion, behavior — whatever "state" means in context) |
| $F$ | the external force being applied (a push, a message, a policy, an influencer's signal) |
| $m$ | mass — here read as resistance to change (inertia, stubbornness, cultural weight) |
| $\dfrac{F}{m}$ | this is just $F=ma$ rearranged — the raw amount of change the force *would* cause with no resonance condition |
| $\omega_F$ | the frequency of the external force/influence (what "wavelength" the push is coming in on) |
| $\omega_i$ | the identity's own natural frequency (what "wavelength" it normally resonates at) |
| $|\omega_F - \omega_i|$ | the size of the mismatch between the two frequencies (always positive, since it's an absolute value) |
| $\epsilon$ | (Greek letter *epsilon*) the identity's tolerance for mismatch — how forgiving it is of an imperfect frequency match; small $\epsilon$ = rigid/hard to influence unless the match is near-perfect, large $\epsilon$ = open/easily influenced even by a loose match |
| $\dfrac{\epsilon}{|\omega_F-\omega_i|+\epsilon}$ | the resonance-scaling factor — equals 1 (full effect) when the mismatch is zero, and shrinks toward 0 (no effect) as the mismatch grows large relative to $\epsilon$ |

**Equation Meaning:** the amount an identity actually changes equals the plain force-over-resistance amount, scaled down by how badly the influence's frequency misses the identity's own frequency — perfect match, full effect; way off, no effect; in between, partial effect.
