The 3rd Rule says:


> When a weaker identity hits a stronger, it will be deflected. When a stronger hits a weaker, it will transfer some of its force to the weaker. 


## $m_A\Delta v_A = -m_B\Delta v_B$, $\ \alpha=\dfrac{S_B}{S_A+S_B}$, $\ S=(m,\sigma,\rho)$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $A$, $B$ | labels for the two identities in the collision (identity A and identity B) |
| $m_A$, $m_B$ | mass of A, mass of B respectively |
| $v_A$, $v_B$ | velocity of A, velocity of B |
| $\Delta v_A$, $\Delta v_B$ | the *change* in velocity of A, and of B, caused by the collision |
| $=-$ | the negative sign — B's change is equal in size but opposite in direction to A's |
| $\alpha$ | (Greek letter *alpha*) the deflection fraction — how much A bounces off rather than pushes through |
| $S_A$, $S_B$ | the total "strength" of A and of B (defined below) — used to figure out who deflects and who transfers force |
| $S$ | the strength vector itself — a bundle of three separate numbers, not just one |
| $m$ (inside $S$) | the mass/material component of strength (physical size, military size, raw resources) |
| $\sigma$ | (Greek letter *sigma*) the resolve/moral-cohesion component of strength (how determined or unified the side is) |
| $\rho$ | (Greek letter *rho*) the persistence component of strength (staying power — economic endurance, cultural durability, ability to keep going) |

**Equation Meaning:** whatever velocity change A undergoes, B undergoes the exact opposite — but which side actually bounces versus pushes through is decided by comparing their strength $S$, and strength itself isn't one number, it's three things (mass, resolve, persistence) bundled together.
