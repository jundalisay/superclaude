The 2nd Rule of Motion says:

> Every moving identity moves in a straight line when alone, and in a curve or a circle when moving with others. When in a circle, it moves away from the center.

This is most obvious with surface tension such as bubbles forming spheres.



## $F_c = mr\omega^2$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $F_c$ | centrifugal force — the outward pull an identity feels while moving in a circle |
| $m$ | mass — or metaphysically, "weight"/stake/commitment of the identity in the group |
| $r$ | radius — distance from the identity to the center it's circling (a leader, an idea, a shared home) |
| $\omega$ | (Greek letter *omega*) angular velocity — how fast the identity is spinning/orbiting around that center |
| $\omega^2$ | omega squared — the spin rate multiplied by itself (this is why doubling the spin quadruples the outward pull) |

**Equation Meaning:** the outward pull equals mass times radius times the square of the spin rate — the faster something spins, or the farther out it sits, the harder it pulls away from center.
