## Atlantis, Halloween, and Ancient Civilizations

The destruction of Atlantis is your biblical flood. 

Many of your Druidic rituals also harken back to some of the Atlantean civilization. 

Your Egyptian and your Central and South American pyramidal structures harken back to the time of Atlantean architecture, for they were begun in that land.

These structures also pertaining to this interaction this evening of your time in terms of the date of your counting: your Halloween, the 3 dates:

1. All Hallows' Eve
2. November 1 All Saints Day
3. November 2 All Souls Day

Those three days are actually the remnant commemoration of the day before, the day of, and the day after the destruction of Atlantis in a sense.

**Energetically speaking, November 1 is actually your true New Year's Day**, the beginning of the cycle of energy that you are now finalizing upon your planet. 

Because the cycle you are now emerging from was begun long ago in Atlantean time, and now you are shifting it.

The idea, as we recognize therefore, your Halloween—your All Hallows' Eve—is that timing when you come, as you say, down to the wire of facing those portions of your consciousness that many of you now, for the several thousand years since the destruction of Atlantis, have submerged into your subconsciousness, have buried deep within you and covered over with the waves of your surface conscious memory.

These ideas now are rising once again from the depths of your being, are coming to the surface to reveal themselves in the knowledge of your connection to the infinite and the reach creation, on the surface of your planet in the society you have now of civilizations and attitudes and creations that are analogous to the heights of the civilization on your planet you call Atlantis.



## The Decision Point

You are very much at the same point once again, almost the same degree of technology. You now have some differences—they did not have, they had some differences you do not have—but you are, relatively speaking, at the same point, the same level of technology. 

And on the verge once again of the same basic decision: do you use it in positive ways and expand and accelerate to the stars, or do you use it in negative ways and once again submerge yourself under the basis of your consciousness?

These decisions are up to you, although we have once again shared with you many times that it is our perception now that you have decided to use it in a positive way, and that is the reason we can be in contact with you at this time. Simply because your vibration is now becoming more and more like the vibration of the worlds that have created peace within their structures of consciousness.

But we know that many of you still like to be frightened, still like to play the ideas out, and this is a very creative thing to do. For if you can find ways to enjoy the fears you have, they can be transformed into positive light. And so in honor of this idea and in willingness to play and participate in your society's customs, we share with you now our version of a little ghost story.


## The House of All Things (Story of Army)

You may adjust the lighting to what you find appropriate, but we will leave that up to you.

It all begins on a world, if you wish, very much like your own. It does not have to be any particular place, it does not really have to be any particular time. And as you say, the subject of the story can be called for now "Army." Army can be, if you wish, male or female, it does not matter.

On Army's world, there is a time and a ritual that everyone must go through before they then undertake, for the remainder of their life, those ideas that they will be utilizing in service to all those things that will represent their passage from what you would call childhood to adulthood, similar in many ways to many rituals you may have upon your world.

This ritual involves the idea of spending some time in a particular structure. This structure is a many-storied structure, and even though it may not have absolute similarities to the idea of buildings upon your planet, it will do in this story to refer to it as a house. Now you may wish to call it a haunted house—that is up to you—but in Army's world it is called the House of All Things.

Everyone knows of it, and it is not without some trepidation that individuals take their turns spending a night within the House of All Things.

And so it came to pass that it was Army's turn to spend that night in the House of All Things. And the teachers and the friends of Army wished Army well, all the while, of course, expressing that they were very glad it was not their turn.

And so, with perhaps some boldness and yet some trepidation, Army set off for the House of All Things, which stood by itself in the very center of a forest, in its own clearing, in its own meadow. Pleasant enough, perhaps, during the day—or so at least Army used to think.

---

### Journey Through the Forest

And as Army traveled through the forest towards the house, it began to be the time of the setting of the sun—dusk, as you call it. And the closer Army got to the House of All Things, the darker and darker it got. And the animals and the trees, of course, through Army's imagination, began to take on slightly different aspects of them, seeming to point the way toward the house but very reluctantly—their branches swaying in the wind, pointing the direction to the house but all the time bending in the wind as if to say:

*"Go back, go back. This is the way you wish to go, but if I were you, I would go back, go back."*

Well, Army knew that Army could go back, but Army also knew this was very important—that there were things to look at, things to face, things to understand if Army were to become what Army really desired to be. Nowhere but in this house could one accelerate so quickly. It was said, though everyone who had been in the house was also told: you may share of your experiences if you wish, but not to the point where you will be living those experiences for everyone else, for it is up to everyone to decide that they themselves must go through this house and have their own experiences.

And as Army walked through the forest, Army dwelt on the many tales that had been told and the many stories that had been shared. Three steps forward, two steps back. Three steps forward, one step back.

---

### Arrival at the House

Eventually, however, Army arrived at the clearing. And there in the clearing at the very center was the House of All Things—multi-storied it stood alone. The wind the only sound, no light coming from anywhere, any window within the house. And Army took one look back at the forest, comparing the idea of going ahead or going back, bolstered up the courage, and walked boldly up to the door.

And just to be polite, gave a little knock, just in case someone might be home—you never know. The door opened slowly, to Army's surprise, but it did not seem to be anyone there. Yet Army took it as an invitation—especially since everyone knew this is what Army was supposed to do now—and stepped in. And the door, you guessed it, closed behind Army ever so gently, not a slam, but with an absolutely precise, definitive, and final *click*.

And in that click there was an echo, an echo of a voice:

*"The way out is up."*

And Army did not really understand this, was not even sure Army had heard this for real. Rubbed Army's ears.

*"My imagination getting to me. I am hearing voices already. I don't know if this was such a good idea. Individuals have said they spent the whole night in this house. I have just arrived, already I'm hearing voices. I will probably never see the dawn. But if this is to be my fate, so be it. If there is anyone around who decides these things, if it is going to be horrible, please at least let it be quick."*

But there was no sound and there was no answer. And all was dark except for here and there the very faint light of a moon shining through this window and that, creating even deeper shadows in contrast to the soft, velvety, violet light.

---

### First Room: The Legs

And Army began to take stock of the surroundings, allowing the eyes to adjust somewhat. But never completely in focus did all of the room become. Army wandered, a creak here, an unknown sound there, a heartbeat.

*"Oh, oh, that must be mine,"* Army said a little louder than usual. *"It is tonight, but it is mine."*

After all, Army sat in the middle of the first room for a very long time, expecting things to come to Army, waiting for what might come with some once-again amount of dread, trepidation, uncertainty, unknowingness. And finally, after what seemed to be many hours, nothing had happened.

*"Well, maybe it's not so bad after all,"* Army said. *"Maybe I could go exploring this old place and find something interesting that no one has ever found. Perhaps all the others were simply too afraid. But if there really is nothing here and I am the first to explore everywhere, I can come out perhaps with many treasures. Who knows what I may find under the floorboards and in the shadows that others have been afraid to go into."*

So Army began to explore. A door here, a door there. Some locked—oh well—some not. Maybe I wish they were. Chose a door, turned it, pushed it open gently—*creak*, an old house—*creak*, a very old house. And into the room Army went. Not much light at all, little cracks here and there, little beams of moonlight, little motes of dust catching the eye.

*"What was that? Oh, just the light. What was that? Oh, just some dust."*

Walked into the room, and the door closed behind with the same definite *click*. Army turned around and tried it, and it did not budge. No going back. The little click echoed: *"Only go up."*

Army turned around to see if there were any other doorways out of the room, and in the shadows could not see any.

*"Only go up? How can I go up? The room is sealed. There is a ceiling. I do not see any other door."*

Army looked around, staying close to the door through which Army came, hoping upon hope that it might open again.

*"Maybe it's just because the house is old. There isn't really anything holding the door shut. Maybe it's just because my hand is trembling and too weak to really pull it from being stuck. Maybe, maybe..."*

And all of a sudden, a slight sound to one side—a sound like a breath—ever so shy. And Army turned and could see nothing at first. And then, slowly, something that made Army recoil against the door. For there seemed to be, far on the other side of the room, very faint, very ghostly, shining with the pale violet light of the moonlight itself, glowing softly, what appeared to be just two legs. Just two legs. As if there could be something standing and watching, but only daring to allow enough of it to materialize to at least allow Army know where it stood. Just two legs connected at the hip with nothing above.

And Army looked a little closer to be sure that Army was seeing what Army thought Army was seeing, and recoiled immediately as the legs took a step forward towards Army. And Army began to perspire heavily, and the heartbeat was ever more loud. And Army turned around and faced the door and began to wish with all the might that was contained in Army's heart that the door would open. And pulled the handle and rattled and rattled and rattled, but it would not give.

And as the last rattle died away, the echo once again: *"The only way out is up."*

And Army turned and looked, and to one side where Army did not see before, there was another door. And Army moved sideways around the wall, staying as far from the legs as possible, and they turned to watch as if they had eyes to observe Army's movement around the room. And Army made sure Army's eyes stayed riveted on those legs so they would not run toward Army, because Army thought if Army could, Army would run right through the wall if that happened.

Army reached the door. The legs seemed content as long as Army did not enter further into the center of the room, to remain where they were, just outlining its domain as it were. Army reached the other door and with one hand opened it and looked, and there were some stairs.

And quick as you can imagine, Army bolted through the door and shut it behind.

---

### Second Room: The Arms

And turned around and saw a very tall staircase, very creaky stairs. And walked up and up and up and up and came to another door, another room.

*"Oh no, not more legs. A house full of legs! What could this mean? How am I supposed to learn from this? I am being scared to death! What is this supposed to teach me? Oh teacher, I wish you were here now. It was you that told me I must go through this if I were to become the Army I want to be. How does this serve me? Can I really last the night?"*

*"Oh well, deep breath. Into the room Army went."*

Didn't see anything immediately. Pretty dark though, just as dark as the other room. Little trickles of light through a very tiny window. Looked around. No legs. All right.

Walked into the center of the room, turned 360 degrees, looked around—no legs.

*"Oh, better and better,"* Army thought.

Explored the room a little bit more, peered into the shadows as far as Army dare. Looked up to the little window, stood looking up the shaft of light as if somehow Army might be able to leap up and squeeze out that little window to get outside into the moonlight where Army knew it would be safer.

And all of a sudden, standing there watching the light, Army felt all of the skin on Army's back as if something had caressed every so gently.

*"No, no, no, just my imagination. The breeze through a crack in the floor."*

And turned around and jumped and stood frozen in the shaft of moonlight. And there, floating, floating nearby in the darkness, glowing ever so gently—a floating pair of arms reaching out, reaching out towards Army, who now began to shake, who began to step back, able to uproot one foot and then the other as if they were glued to the floor and becoming stuck again and not taking another step. And staring and staring at the arms that reached out, groping, grasping for Army.

And just beyond the arms to one side, Army caught in the faintest glow of the light another door. Army wasted no time. Army ran and did not stop, the beating of Army's heart so loud, until the next door was slammed securely behind.

---

### Third Room: The Heart

And this time, by now Army was becoming weak and frightened and wishing there were a window large enough, for now Army was certain Army would jump even if it meant Army's death, for Army did not want to spend another moment.

And yet once again, in the slamming of that door and the clicking of the lock, there was the small voice:

*"The way out is up."*

*"I'm going mad,"* Army thought. *"Truly mad. Perhaps I will simply become a resident of this house. Perhaps this is how this house has become so haunted—I am seeing all the mad people that have been here before me who have never escaped. And neither will I. Woe with me."*

But there ahead of Army was yet again another staircase.

*"Well, if I am going to die, it might as well be now."*

And up the stairs Army climbed. And with each step, Army's legs became heavier and heavier and heavier, until at the very top Army wondered why the floor underneath did not give way and Army didn't go crashing through.

Army looked at the door at the top of the stairs, the same bright brass knob waiting for the touch of Army's hand, as if it had always been waiting for the touch of Army's hand to turn it. And with leaden arms, Army reached and felt the smooth coldness—the coldness that Army thought that any moment would signal the coldness that Army's body would be in death if Army walked through that door.

And turned it. *Click*. A very, very old door, very rusty. What strength Army had left was necessary, all of it, to muster to push the door open, for Army knew it was the only way—there was no going back.

And into the room Army went. And the door closed behind—*click*—and the echo died away. But Army did not hear, for Army's senses now were draining, and Army's heart was in the throat. For ahead of Army, floating in the center of the dark room, was only a heart—a beating heart.

Army could not think. Army could not move. Army felt dark death wrapping around and covering Army's eyes. Army withdrew and curled into a little ball on the floor and felt the eternal night descend.

---

### The Turning Point

But then—but then—in the very depths of Army's despair and fear and darkness, that little voice echoed again:

*"Up, Army. The way out is up."*

*"I am on the top floor,"* Army cried. *"Up? Army, up? Way out is up? What do you want from me?"*

Army cried, yet Army found strength coming back. A strength that if anything, even if it was anger, even if it was defiance, that Army's predicament was allowing Army to regain senses. And rising slowly, Army saw the heart, and the heart beat and glowed fiercely and brightly. And Army did not feel as scared—God has scared—and felt a warmth from the heart.

*"Who are you?"* said Army.

*"Up,"* said the heart.

*"I do not understand,"* said Army.

*"You will,"* said the heart. *"You will."*

The heart vanished. And the door behind Army clicked open. And Army saw light from below in a house that had had no light up to that point. And descended the staircase and walked into the room, peering around the corner, of course, looking for the arm that had groped for Army's throat, and saw nothing.

And walked into the room, saw nothing. The room was lit. Looked one wall, another wall, and the third wall—and froze. And then recovered. For there on the far, far wall was a mirror. And it was only Army's arms in the moonlight that had scared Army.

And went to the other door and down the stairs as fast as you could imagine into the other room, where there was now light. And once again the mirror—the mirror on the far wall. It had only been Army's legs that had trembled and turned as Army turned around the room to seek escape, shining the reflection of the moonlight off of Army's legs into the mirror.

And Army went to the front door and it opened easily. And out of the House of All Things to stand in the meadow and look at it from afar. And the night was glorious, and the moon shone brightly, and Army was happy Army had lived.

---

### The Mystery of the Heart

And all of a sudden, a thought struck Army:

*"But wait. In the first room had been the reflection in the mirror of my legs. In the second room had been the reflection in the mirror of my arms. But no mirror can reflect a heart. What was that? What was that, then? There was a spirit. I wonder if I'll ever know."*

And it came to pass that Army lived a full life, doing all the things that Army desired to do, in being of service, in joy and love. And now and then pondered back to the House of All Things and the heart, and wondered but found no answer. Could not understand how the house had been able to show a heart, to see through Army's body, to show Army's heart—if that is what it truly was.

---

### Army's Passing

And so it came to pass that in the living of a full life, it came time for Army to die. And in passing from the physical to the spirit world, Army was met by Army's teacher, who had preceded before.

*"How nice to see you again. And now we are spirits. And hearkening back to the time of the House of All Things, I can see in that sense that spirits are not things to be feared."*

*"And funny you should mention that,"* said the teacher. *"Funny you should mention the House of All Things. For just as it was in physical life, just as the House of All Things represented a passage that each living being must go through, the House of All Things is something that you as a novice spirit must always play a part in serving."*

*"How so? You mean now that I'm dead, I have to go back there?"*

*"Just for a moment or two. Just to be as a guide of service in love and in light. For it is the House of All Things, and by definition it bridges both worlds. And it is a doorway and a passage that not only the living in life, but the living in death must also participate in."*

*"Very well, I will do what I must,"* said Army.

*"So go,"* said the teacher. *"There is someone waiting now."*

---

### Army as the Heart

And so Army went and entered the spirit side of the House of All Things, but of course not through the front door—through the top, into the attic. And as soon as Army was in place, the sound of a click. And Army turned and saw Army—Army in life being viewed by Army in death.

And Army understood.

*"I am the heart. I saw. I am the guide. I saw so long ago in life my own self in the future, radiating the love—the self-love it would take for me to live the life I desired to live. I saw my own heart truly, but not reflected in any mirror of physical design. Reflected in the mirror of spirit to give myself the reminder that I am loved."*
