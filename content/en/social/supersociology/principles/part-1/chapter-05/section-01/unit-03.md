The changing nature of social cycles means that one class is always dominant just as a wave has a peak that is not flat. 

A society with a dominant worker class will likely push for worker interests, just as one dominated by traders will push for business interests. This leads to liberalism, and neoliberalism respectively. 

## Single Dominant

| # | W-Wa-T-Tr | Name | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1-3-2-2 | **Military Junta** | Labor suppressed, force dominant, everything else just ticking along |
| 3 | 1-2-3-2 | **Technocracy or Scholastic Bureaucracy** | Ideas rule, workers are undervalued, force is modest |
| 4 | 1-2-2-3 | **Plutocracy or Corporate Oligarchy** | Trade dominant, labor devalued — rule by capital, not by the people who make things |
| 5 | 3-1-2-2 | **Socialist Republic** | Labor exalted, military minimized — the workers' commonwealth |
| 6 | 2-1-3-2 | **Platonic Republic** | Philosopher-rule: ideas high, force deliberately low |
| 7 | 2-1-2-3 | **Democratic Socialism** | trade allowed to lead, but force restrained |
| 8 | 3-2-1-2 | **Populist Nationalism** | Worker-glorifying, anti-intellectual, moderate force and trade |
| 9 | 2-3-1-2 | **Fascist Corporatism or Praetorianism** | Force rules for its own sake — no ideology, no worker cult, just the strongman |
| 10 | 2-2-1-3 | **Mercantilism** | Trade is king, thinkers are irrelevant to the state |
| 11 | 3-2-2-1 | **State Socialism** | Labor exalted, markets suppressed, force at a normal level |
| 12 | 2-3-2-1 | **Feudalism** | force and land dominate, commerce stifled |
| 13 | 2-2-3-1 | **Theocratic Republic** | Thought/faith elevated, markets suppressed, force ordinary |




### Dual-Dominant

*Two classes hold maximum power (3) while two are suppressed (1).*

| Score `[Wor, War, Thi, Tra]` | Societal Model Name | Core Dynamic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3 - 3 - 1 - 1** | **Communism** | labor and force fused into the state, thought and markets suppressed |
| **3 - 1 - 3 - 1** | **Anarcho-Syndicalism / Utopian Socialism** | Direct worker ownership guided by radical intellectual design, with no standing military or commercial market. |
| **3 - 1 - 1 - 3** | **Neoliberalism** | labor and markets drive everything, force and ideology minimal |
| **1 - 3 - 3 - 1** | **Totalitarian Theocracy** | force and dogma fused, commerce and labor crushed |
| **1 - 3 - 1 - 3** | **Colonialism** | Force + trade extract value; labor and ideas of the governed don't matter |
| **1 - 1 - 3 - 3** | **Laissez-Faire Technocratic Capitalism** | Ideas and markets fused, labor and military both minimized — Silicon Valley as a state |

Syndicalism (3-1-2-2) is labor's moment: production is glorified, but the military is starved of resources, since a worker's state has no appetite for standing armies. **The near-dominant societies** are where one class leads but a second class has real weight, producing hybrids. Military Junta (1-3-2-2) suppresses labor while force rules — but thought and trade limp along at survivable levels, unlike a pure theocracy or pure colonial regime, because the junta needs functioning bureaucrats and merchants even as it distrusts the masses. Technocracy (1-2-3-2) similarly undervalues workers but elevates expertise, producing something like a managerial state run by credentialed planners. Plutocracy (1-2-2-3) is capital's rule without capital's ideology — trade dominates, but there's no NeoLiberal philosophy backing it, just money buying policy directly. Authoritarian Populism (3-2-1-2) glorifies the worker rhetorically while distrusting intellectuals, backed by an ordinary (not dominant) military — the strongman who claims to speak for the common man. State Socialism (3-2-2-1) is labor-led but keeps force at a normal level and starves the market, distinguishing it from Communism's fused labor-military core. Feudalism (2-3-2-1) and the Theocratic Republic (2-2-3-1) round this group out: the former lets land and force dominate while commerce is stifled, the latter lets doctrine dominate while trade is stifled — two different ways of suppressing the merchant class in favor of hierarchy or faith.

**The balanced society**, 2-2-2-2, is the anomaly. Social Democracy is the equilibrium every constitution claims to be reaching for — labor, force, ideas, and markets held in rough parity, none permitted to peak. It's rare in practice because equilibrium is unstable: any external shock (war, famine, a charismatic leader) tips one class upward and the society drifts toward one of the other eighteen configurations. In the cyclical view, 2-2-2-2 isn't a resting state so much as a transition point — the moment a wave is neither cresting nor troughing.

**The fused-extreme societies** are the ideologically "purest" — two classes surge to 3 while the other two collapse to 1, producing doctrinaire systems rather than messy governance. Communism (3-3-1-1) fuses labor and force into the party-state, crushing both thought and trade as bourgeois distractions. Totalitarian Theocracy (1-3-3-1) fuses force and dogma instead, crushing labor's autonomy and commerce alike in service of doctrine. Neoliberalism (3-1-1-3) fuses labor and markets — work and enterprise as twin virtues — while minimizing both the state's coercive arm and its intellectual class, on the theory that the market thinks for itself. Utopian Socialism (3-1-3-1) fuses labor and philosophy into something like a monastery-commune: workers and thinkers exalted together, force and trade both rejected as corrupting. Colonialism (1-3-1-3) fuses force and trade to extract value from a periphery, with the labor and ideas of the governed treated as irrelevant. And Technocratic Capitalism (1-1-3-3) fuses ideas and markets — a Silicon Valley state where expertise and enterprise govern jointly, while ordinary labor and the military are both marginal to the project.

**The pattern beneath the pattern** is that intensity doesn't just measure how strong a class is — it measures what the *other* classes are forced to give up to let it rise. Every "3" is bought with at least one "1" somewhere else. Read this way, the sequence from Syndicalism through Technocratic Capitalism isn't a random list of regime-types but a map of trade-offs: which class a society has chosen to sacrifice in order to let another one peak. History, on this model, isn't a single line of progress but a wave function cycling through these nineteen peaks and troughs — each stable only until the sacrifice it demands becomes unbearable, and the wave breaks toward the next configuration. -->
