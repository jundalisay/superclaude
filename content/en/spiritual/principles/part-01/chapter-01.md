We classify action as the 1st path of spirituality as mirroring the worker class and 1st Rule of motion as commonality. 

This is called Karma Yoga.


## Karma Yoga

Karma means action in Sanskrit. 


In the Bhagavad Gītā, **Karma Yoga** is the path of *action without attachment*. Its core principle is: **“Your right is to work, but never to the fruit of work.”** The practitioner acts fully, faithfully, and with all their heart, not to gain praise, profit, status, or even spiritual reward, but as an offering—sacred, loving, and surrendered to the Divine. Every action becomes worship when done with love and detachment from results.


## Christianity: Service as the Very Heart of Faith

We classify Christianity as karma yoga because of its core teaching that centers on selfless service, loving labor, and doing good without attachment to reward.

Jesus Christ summarized the entire law in two commands: love God, and **love your neighbor as yourself**. He taught that true greatness lies not in power or rank, but in serving—saying, *“Whoever would be great among you must be your servant… even as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.”*

This teaching mirrors Karma Yoga perfectly:

- **Work as worship**: Whatever you do—in daily labor, kindness, or care for others—do it as unto God, not for human praise. As the Apostle Paul wrote: *“Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men.”*
- **Service without attachment**: Give, help, and love without expecting anything in return. *“Give to the one who begs from you, and do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you.”* The reward belongs to God alone; your part is only to give.
- **Love in action**: Faith is proven by what you do. *“Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.”* Compassion, care, forgiveness, and humble labor are the very language of love.

## The Shared Essence: Love in Action

Karma Yoga and Christian service both arrive at the same truth: **you draw near to God not only by praying or believing, but by loving and serving.** In both:
- Action is not separate from spirituality—it is spirituality made visible.
- The ego is softened when you labor for others rather than for yourself.
- The highest form of devotion is to give your life, your work, and your care, freely and without condition.
