**Stoicism is the perfect Western expression of Rāja Yoga**, sharing the same unshakable foundation: discipline transforms consciousness, and simplicity releases the spirit.

## Austerity: Training the Soul

Austerity—known in Yoga as *tapas*, or spiritual heat through restraint—is central to both paths. It means voluntarily living simply, without excess, so you do not become dependent on comfort. Yoga teaches fasting, silence, and simplicity; Stoicism practiced **voluntary hardship**: sleeping on a hard bed, wearing plain clothes, going without food or warmth on purpose—not out of necessity, but to build strength.

The Stoic philosopher Epictetus asked: *“When you are attached to pleasure, wealth, or praise, remember that you have enslaved yourself.”* Austerity strips away the non‑essentials so you can see clearly: **you are not your body, your wealth, or your reputation. You are consciousness, free and eternal.**

## Poverty: The Wealth of Freedom

Rāja spirituality embraces **poverty of spirit**—freedom from attachment to possessions—not because things are evil, but because they are chains. The Yogi owns nothing, needs little, and holds the whole universe as their true wealth.

Stoicism calls this **indifference to externals**: you may have things, but you do not cling to them. The ideal is to be so self‑sufficient that even if all is taken away, you remain calm, complete, and happy. As the Stoic Seneca wrote: *“He is rich not who has much, but who needs little.”* This is pure Rāja wisdom: **when you own nothing in your heart, nothing can be taken from you.**

## One Path, Two Names

Rāja Yoga and Stoicism are separated by time, place, and words—but not by truth. Both declare: **master your impulses, simplify your life, accept what comes, and align yourself with the Universal Order.** Discipline is the path, austerity the training, and freedom the prize. Stoicism is Rāja Yoga in the language of the West—practical, noble, and unyielding.
