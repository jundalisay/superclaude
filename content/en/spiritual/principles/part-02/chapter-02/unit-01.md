We classify Islam as Rāja Yoga as it places immense weight on structured practice, self‑governance, and strict adherence to principles as the surest way to purify the heart and unite with the Divine.

## What Is Rāja Yoga?

## Islam: Discipline as the Path to Surrender


The very word **Islam** means “surrender” or “submission”—to the will of the One God. But this surrender is not passive; it is active, structured, and lived. That structure is found in the **Shariah**—the “Way” or divine law—which encompasses every part of life: faith, worship, conduct, family, society, and personal ethics.

The “Five Pillars” and the broader code of conduct function much like the *yamas* and *niyamas* of Rāja Yoga:
- **Faith** as the unshakable foundation of truth
- **Prayer five times daily** as a regular, disciplined return to the Divine—regardless of mood, feeling, or circumstance
- **Fasting during Ramadan** as training in self‑control, conquering appetite, and purifying body and soul
- **Charity** as the moral regulation of how one uses wealth and relates to others
- **Rules of conduct** governing speech, food, relationships, honesty, and justice—all intended to shape character and align life with the Good.

## The Common Thread

In both Rāja Yoga and this understanding of Islam, **rules are not burdens—they are liberating.** Just as an athlete trains by strict routine to excel, the soul follows discipline to transcend its lower nature. The many rules and rituals are not ends in themselves; they are tools to:
- Tame the ego and its impulses
- Free the mind from chaos and distraction
- Make every action an offering, not just a habit
- Build a life where the Divine is remembered constantly, not only in prayer.

Both traditions teach that **uncontrolled desire and scattered awareness are the greatest obstacles.** By adhering to clear, consistent discipline—whether called *yama/niyama* or *Shariah*—the practitioner gradually rises above the “lower self” and becomes capable of true, wholehearted surrender and union.
