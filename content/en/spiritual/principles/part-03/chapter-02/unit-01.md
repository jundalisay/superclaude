We classify **Vedic philosophy** as Jñāna Yoga as they are the same truth, one as the teaching and the other as the way to live it.

Vedic philosophy is the body of knowledge; Jñāna Yoga is the practice of that knowledge. They are the vision and the seeing, inseparable from one another.

## What Is Vedic Philosophy?

*Veda* means **knowledge**—not ordinary information, but direct, unchanging wisdom concerning the nature of reality, the Self, and the Divine. 

Found in the ancient hymns, ritual texts, and especially the **Upanishads**—the philosophical core of the Vedas—Vedic philosophy asks the ultimate questions: *Who am I? What is this universe? What lies beyond time and death?*

Its central insight is profound: **There is only One Reality.** The individual Self (*Ātman*) and the Universal Reality (*Brahman*) are not different—they are the same. All separation, all diversity, all distinctions are appearances created by the mind, not by truth. To know this is to be free.

## Vedic Philosophy = Jñāna Yoga in Form

Vedic philosophy is the very substance of Jñāna Yoga. Every core teaching of Jñāna Yoga comes directly from the Vedas:

- **The Oneness of All**: The Vedas declare, *“All this is Brahman.”* Jñāna Yoga lives this truth.
- **The Reality of Ātman**: The Vedas teach that you are not the body, not the mind, but the immortal Self. Jñāna Yoga meditates upon this until it is known.
- **Freedom through Knowledge**: The Vedas say, *“When a person sees the Self as Lord, the knots of the heart are broken, doubts are destroyed, and all karma dissolves.”* This is the very definition of Jñāna Yoga: **liberation comes not by works, but by knowing what you are.**

## Not Belief—Realization

Vedic philosophy is not a creed to accept; it is a map to be verified. Likewise, Jñāna Yoga is not a set of beliefs; it is the practice of applying Vedic wisdom to your own consciousness until you experience the truth yourself. **Veda is the knowledge; Jñāna is the act of knowing.** One is the voice, the other is the hearing.
